# Bram's debt (decided 19 November 2026)

Gary asked "what debt exactly is he taking?" and, of seven ideas, chose
**1** (a life for a life), **3** (the debt that grows) and **7** (Bram's last
duel). This is the whole of it, for every script that touches Bram from here
on. Only the Prologue's lines are in the game so far.

## What it is

- **The child was meant to die that night.** His death was already written in
  Morrowen's book (she keeps the book of the dead), and the hooded hunter had
  come to collect it.
- **A death can't be cancelled, only moved.** The Last Page saves a life only
  by writing another name in its place. Bram writes his own: "Write it in my
  name, then." It isn't taken that night. It falls due when the Age ends.
- **A moved death gathers interest.** It's never paid in full by the one
  name. Over twenty years it grows, until it's more than Bram alone can pay,
  and the people close to the boy start paying it too. This answers
  Morrowen's opening: "Most spend a lifetime paying interest on a life they
  never asked for."
- **The hooded figure is its collector.** It burned in the Last Page's light,
  and it was standing on the rim again as Bram walked away. It isn't
  finished.
- **When it comes for him, the player plays Bram's last duel.** As in the
  Prologue, but he can't win this time. He fights to buy the hero time ("Buy
  me some time. That's all I need." comes back), plays the Last Page one last
  time, and dies paying what he owes. The hero takes his deck and the Last
  Page.

## Where it goes next (to be written into the scripts, for Gary's yes)

- **Lantern Night, the last night of the Age.** What's already there reads as
  the interest falling due: Tam killed, the inn burned, Ashford's houses taken
  by the House. Morrowen's Ledger can say so, one line, in her voice.
- **Through the game, the hooded figure at the edges.** Seen once in each part,
  never explained: across a square, on a far ridge, at the back of a crowd,
  always where Bram is. Players work out that it's waiting for him.
- **Bram knows.** He never says it outright until near the end. He's slower to
  get up, and he gives the hero things as if he won't need them. The talk
  about Kaethis that was planned for Part 3 moves into his last scenes.
- **His last duel.** The hooded figure comes for him. The player plays Bram,
  as on the night of the White Sky: his high cards, Kaethis's, the Last Page.
  He can't win. He holds it off long enough for the hero to get clear, plays
  the Last Page one last time, and pays.
- **After.** The hero carries Bram's deck and the Last Page. Whether the debt
  is now fully paid, or some of it's still owed, is a question for the end of
  the game.
