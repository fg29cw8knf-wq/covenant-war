# The Covenant War · The return to Ashford (draft v1, for Gary's yes)

_5 November 2026. Written by Claude for Gary to read before anything is built.
Nothing here is in the game yet. It follows Corin's return
(`v8_corins_return.md`) and leans on Interlude A (`v8_interlude_a.md`)._

**How to read it.** As in v7 and the other v8 scripts: **Name:** is a line;
_(italics in brackets)_ are what we see and what the player does; **[GAME]** is
what the game does that isn't a line, and anything it tells the player is in
brackets on screen; **Thought:** is the player's own thought. **Every line
waits for a tap.** In a menu, the questions not yet asked come back after each
answer, and one choice moves on. A line that depends on an earlier choice is
marked _(if ...)_.

**Where it sits.** Straight after Corin's return, as a short interlude of its
own, before the fourth kingdom. Late summer of the Turning year: the barley
green and high in the fields, and every field the House's.

**What brings the player back.** Corin. If they signed his bond, they're
taking him home to his mam because he can't do the road alone. If they didn't,
Bram turns the wagon for the valley because somebody should see her who isn't
a letter. Either way, half a year after they went out through the tunnel.

**What it changes.** Three things, small and real. Tilly lets the player off
their promise. Tam's account is closed the village's way, under the Great
Lantern, or quietly on his stone: the one choice. And (if Corin came home) his
Cinderpup wakes for him for a heartbeat. Bram finds that what he came down to
the cellar for is gone.

**How long it plays.** 15 to 20 minutes. The green is free to walk; whoever's
spoken to, in any order; then Bram at the inn; then dusk.

|---|---|---|

## Writers' notes (for Gary and Claude only, never shown in the game)

**Why here, and not as its own interlude anywhere.** Corin's return gives the
visit its reason, whichever way the player chose: you take him home, or you go
to his mam yourself. It also puts the House's paper side by side: the player
has just seen ASHFORD · 20 on a rack in a strongroom, and now sees the twenty
doors. It's half a year on, long enough for Tilly's "every day" to have been
broken and for the place to have settled into what it is now. And it comes
before Interlude B, so when the player stands at Morne's desk they've seen
what the desk did. (The other way: its own interlude after any kingdom from
the second on, without Corin. It would need its own reason to come home, and
loses the bakery: Question 1.)

**What Ashford is now.** Twenty-two roofs, all standing, all the House's but
two: the mill (Tam's note, sent back marked paid on Morne's word in C7, "Profit
from a death looks like exactly what it is") and the bakery (deeded back to
Maudie for Corin's twenty-year bond). The only two free roofs in Ashford were
paid for with a life and with twenty years of one. The inn burned after the
search, because it was the one roof that wasn't the House's (Krell says so in
Interlude A); it squares C7's "The inn we burned" with v7's houses kept
standing. Nell took the mill because the valley needs one, and grinds the
House's barley because there's no other.

**Does a debt end at the grave?** The pantheon's line: Ironvault says no,
Ashford's lanterns say yes, and Tam Hayes is the test. The House marked his
note paid, as if he'd paid it. Nell's answer is the heart of the visit: "He
didn't pay it. He gave it." The Lantern is the village saying so out loud,
under the House's nose.

**The choice, and what it sets.** `ar_lantern_lit` or `ar_lantern_stone`. The
point is the one from Hale's Orders: people paying for standing with you, and
whether you let them.
- **Lit:** Ashford's Standing rises; Ellowen's (the Lantern Keeper, whose
  festival it is) too, which Graces her cards. Mr Hendry's list comes back in
  Part 2: the House puts a penny a week on the rent of every name on it, and
  nobody on it complains. In Part 2, when the player crosses Tollin's bridge
  and Tam is on the far side, he says, "Somebody lit me a lantern. I found the
  road."
- **On the stone:** nobody's rent goes up. Tam, at the bridge: "Nell left me a
  light. It got me as far as here." (The bridge is Part 2's quest; the pantheon
  gives it to Tollin.)

**Bram's cellar.** In Interlude A Krell says, "Then you'll have the page": the
page sealed at Ashford's gate twenty years ago, proof that Bram paid. Here he
goes down for "something with a seal on it", and it's gone: Krell's search
took it. Interlude B can pick it up (Morne has it), or cut the beat and Bram
simply stands in his cellar. Only said if the player asks.

**Tilly.** The promise was "every day", and the player couldn't keep it. The
moth isn't grey because it's been with the one it likes; Tilly lets them off.
It flies only for the player, as it always has.

**Corin, if he came home.** The first time he feels what the page cost. His
answer to "who taught you that?" in the strongroom comes back as the first
thing he says on the wall. His Cinderpup, grey since the House, wakes for a
heartbeat: a Spark goes grey if it's kept from the one it likes, and the one it
likes has just come back. He stays in Ashford for a month and comes to find
the player in Part 2 about the note.

**Callbacks used, all from v7 or v3.** Hob's "Barley's in. Roof's patched."
Nell's "Bench is still here. So am I." (turned to the stones). Corin's "Don't.
I'm not sulking." Bram's bar that never needed wiping. The name written in the
frost (here, in soot). The look back, the other way: walked away from this
time, with Bram's voice instead of his hand. Bram's three lines at Tam's stone
are v3's campfire lines ("Tam ground my flour ...", "He stood there and lied to
that woman's face for you ...", "I'd do it again tomorrow."), which v7 cut with
the campfire; they find their place here.
