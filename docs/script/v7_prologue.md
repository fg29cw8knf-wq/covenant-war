# The Covenant War · Master Script v7 · The Prologue (game build)

_24 October 2026. Gary's **Master Script v6 – Complete Prologue** (the PDF beside
this file, `v6_master_script_prologue.pdf`, and its text in
`v6_master_script_prologue.txt`) with the changes Gary asked for the same night:
four deck boxes, the seven story notes he sent ("give the player more time with
Ashford", "Corin's betrayal must be heartbreaking", Bram's continuity, why Bram
is still in Ashford when Krell comes, holding back the Last Seal, Bram's lesson
woven with his past, the player's own feelings, the mirror without amnesia) and
the moment after Tam's death. The game is built from this file._

**How to read it.** Everything not marked is Gary's v6, word for word.
**(new)** marks a line or scene Claude wrote for v7. **(changed)** marks a v6 line
Claude altered, with the reason in brackets. **[GAME]** marks what the game does
that isn't a line (instructions shown in brackets on screen, never in a
character's voice; a tutorial step; a menu tab opening). `{name}` is the name the
player writes. The hero is a young man, played as **Player A** in every scene and
film. Every line waits for a tap unless it has a recording, which plays and moves
on by itself (a tap skips it). In a conversation hub the questions not yet asked
come back after each answer; a "Progress" option moves on.

**Losing a story duel** (unchanged from v3): the opponent's winning line, then
Morrowen takes the story back with one line, never the same twice running ("No.
That isn't how it went." / "No. I have it written differently." / "Not like
that. Again." / "That isn't what's in my ledger."); after a second loss, only a
page turning back, and Again / Change deck first.

## Writers' notes (new; for Gary and Claude only, never shown in the game)

**What the two halves of the Last Seal are (proposed, for Gary's yes).** The
Covenant is a loan: the gods borrowed Veyl from whatever was here first, and the
Covenant is the agreement, signed at the bottom with a seal. It was signed aeons
ago (Gary, 24 October), and when it was signed the seal was broken in two, so that
no god could ever foreclose alone, however many Ages they held the throne. **The
first half** is the blank card: the signature space itself, which fell at the
Turning and came to the one thing in Veyl it could recognise. **The second half**
is the protagonist. Twenty years ago, the night the sky burned white over the
Shards, the broken seal's impression was pressed into a newborn: the ash-grey
mark on his collarbone is the wax, not a scar. That is why the gods have no
mortgage on his soul and Ironvault has no receipt for his birth, why magic bends
toward him, and why the card can't simply be taken from him (it has to be staged
and lost in a sworn duel, or he has to die). Whoever brings the two halves
together holds the right to sign the world over. Nobody says this in the
Prologue; Bram knows most of it.

**The Covenant and the Ages (Gary, 24 October).** The Covenant was formed aeons
ago, and there have been many reigns since. One Age is two thousand years, as the
opening movie says. A god who wins the Turning again keeps the throne for the next
Age as well, so some have sat on it for four or six thousand years running. **The
Age that ends at midnight was Solmaris's** (Gary): her first, only two thousand
years, and she is determined to win the Turning and have another two thousand.
(It fits the movie's "One god rules for one Age: two thousand years", Bram's "Two
thousand years. Wonder who'll be sitting up there tomorrow." and "The Age of
Solmaris is ending"; it's why the Sun's Court in Solhaven fights so hard later.)

**Bram's timeline.** Came down off the Shards twenty years ago with the baby and a
coat full of ash-smelling gold; paid Aurex Morne four thousand crowns that same
week for the inn outright (the only roof Ironvault doesn't own), cut the tunnel and
its twenty-two Ward marks that year, and built the Thornwood shelter. Taught the
boy to duel in the cellar with half-spent Sparks and his own hand over the boy's:
the rules, never the deep draw. "Forty years before" the Herald: he named the
Unsworn deck in the life he won't talk about.

**Corin's road.** The clerkship is for his mam's note, not for the warm rooms. He
gave the protagonist the best button off his coat. When he comes back bound to
Ironvault, his mind altered, he is not simply someone else: in a later trap,
reaching for the white card, he calls the protagonist "Sprout", and stops himself;
