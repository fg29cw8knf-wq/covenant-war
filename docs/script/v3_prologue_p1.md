# Master Script v3: Prologue P1 (Ashford, before midnight)

_A plain copy of Gary's "Master Script v3" Claude Doc (Movie & Prologue tab), from "How to read the script" to the start of P2 (midnight), taken on 18 October 2026 so a coding session without the doc can build from it. v3 was approved and locked on 18 October and replaces the Spoken Script (`p1_ashford.md` is the older v2 copy). The doc is the master: if they differ, the doc wins._

_What the game does with each line is in `scripts/world/areas/ashford.gd` (the P1 part, above the films) and `scripts/world/areas/ashford_p1.gd`._

## How to read the script

**Speaker:** is a line of dialogue; a note in brackets after a name says when it plays and is never shown. _Italics_ are what we see and what the player does. **Objective:** lines appear on screen as the current task, with the button to press in brackets. **The player's choice:** lists what the player can say. `{name}` is the name the player gives. Only Bram says "Sprout". The player is never given a gendered word. Lines marked (V) are voiced. A dialogue box holds two sentences at most; Ashford speaks a light, rural Northern English.

**Losing a story duel:** the opponent's line for winning plays, then Morrowen (V) takes the story back with one line, never the same one twice running: "No. That isn't how it went." / "No. I have it written differently." / "Not like that. Again." / "That isn't what's in my ledger." She speaks once per duel; after a second loss there is only the sound of a page turning back, and two buttons: Again, and Change deck first.

**Sworn wagers in side stories** are played once, and the story follows the result. The swear screen shows the opponent's duel rule and says "A sworn wager. Win or lose, it stands." with Swear now and Not yet. Closing the app mid-duel always resumes the duel; it never counts as a loss.

## Prologue: The Sigilfall

### P1 · The attic and the taproom

_Black. The festival drum from the movie keeps beating._

**Caption:** Ashford. The last night of the Age.

_The inn's attic: low beams, a straw bed, a cracked mirror. Through the little window, the green is filling up with lanterns. The player wakes to the drum and looks in the mirror._

**Narration:** Nobody alive has seen an Age end. So Ashford has decided it ends with cider.

**Objective:** Choose your look. (Tap a face.)

_The taproom. Bram is stacking paper lanterns into a crate. The shelves behind the bar are bare: the good tankards are already out on the green._

**Bram:** There you are. Thought you'd sleep through the end of the world.

**Bram:** Once every two thousand years. We're not going to be late for it.

**The player's choice:** What happens when it ends? / I dreamt about the sky again.

**Bram (what happens):** The gods settle who sits the Throne next. We hang lanterns and drink to whoever it is. Nobody's ever asked us to do more than that.

**Bram (the sky):** _(He stops stacking, just for a moment.)_ The white one?

_The player says nothing. Bram picks up the next lantern._

**Bram (the sky):** Well. It's still up there. Crate.

**Bram:** Corin's been telling the whole green he'll duel at the Sun Court one day. Solhaven. Under the gold roofs.

**The player's choice:** One day I will too. / Corin says a lot of things.

**Bram (one day):** You won't. Nobody from Ashford goes to Solhaven, and Solhaven's never once come looking for Ashford. Let's keep it that way.

**Bram (Corin says):** He does. One day he'll go, and you'll stay and run this place, and I'll die happy.

**Bram:** Lanterns to the green, then. You know the way.

**Objective:** Carry the lantern crate to the green. (Tap the ground to walk.)

**Bram (as you reach the door):** And if you need to stop, stop. The world'll wait.

_On screen, the square in the corner glows once: it pauses and saves._

_Bram follows you out. A street of thatched cottages and bunting; the green beyond, with stalls, a fiddler, children with paper lanterns and the Great Lantern on its tall pole. Nailed to the chapel door, a row of identical notices, each sealed with a coin crossed with a key._

**Bram (if you dawdle):** The green. Big pole, middle of it. You've walked there every day of your life.

**Bram (at the green):** Top hook's yours. It always has been.

**Objective:** Hang the last lantern on the Great Lantern's pole. (Walk up to the glowing hook and tap it.)

**Bram (once it's hung):** Good. Now go and be useful.

**Bram:** Tam first. He's been carrying a letter round all day like it's hot.

**Bram:** Nell's on the bench by the well, if you've time. Then Corin, and get the Lantern Duel over with.

**Narration:** He glances up at the stars the way he'd watch a stranger's hands.

**Bram:** Go on.

**Objective:** Find Tam at the hay cart. (Walk up to him and tap Talk.)

### P1 · Bram, when you talk to him

**Bram (before you've seen Tam):** Tam, Sprout. Hay cart. Go on.

**Bram (after Tam, before Nell):** Nell's by the well. Sit with her a minute. She'll not ask twice.

**Bram (once you've seen Tam):** Corin's been warming up an audience for an hour. Go and spoil it for him. Whoever wins lights the Great Lantern at midnight.

**Bram (always, last):** And stay where I can see you tonight.

### P1 · Tam and the note

_Tam is working a cider barrel off the hay cart. A folded letter sticks out of his shirt pocket, sealed with a coin crossed with a key._

**Tam:** Evening. Take the other end of this, would you? Stall wants it before the dancing.

**Objective:** Carry the barrel to the stall with Tam. (Tap the stall.)

_You set it down together. Tam doesn't let go of the barrel straight away._

**Tam:** You read, don't you. Bram had you doing the inn's books at ten.

**Tam:** Ironvault's man left this at the mill. Nell does my reading, as a rule. Rather she didn't do this one.

_He holds out the letter._

**Objective:** Read Tam's letter. (Tap it.)

_The letter, on screen:_ To T. Hayes, miller, of Ashford. The note of eleven hundred crowns secured on the mill, its stones and its house falls due in full at the close of the present Age. Ironvault thanks you for your custom.

**Tam:** Well? New moon, their man said. That's three weeks off. Time enough to sort something.

**The player's choice:** It says tonight. / It says the new moon.

**Tam (tonight):** ...Tonight.

_He folds the letter very small and puts it back in his pocket._

**Tam (tonight):** Forty years I've ground this village's flour. Never missed a day.

**Tam (tonight):** Don't tell Nell. Not tonight. She's been looking forward to this since harvest.

**Tam (new moon):** There. Three weeks. That's a harvest supper and a bit of luck.

**Tam (new moon):** Thank you. Don't tell Nell I asked. She thinks I'm cleverer than I am.

**Objective:** Duel Corin on the green. Nell's on the bench by the well, if you've time.

**Tam (afterwards, if you told him):** I'm going to dance with my wife. Ironvault can watch.

**Tam (afterwards, if you didn't):** Three weeks. I'll have Nell dancing before then, knees or no knees.

### P1 · Nell by the well (side story)

_Nell sits on the bench by the well with a shawl round her shoulders, watching the dancing. She pats the bench beside her._

**Nell:** Sit with me, love. My knees have given up dancing, and Tam's given up asking.

**Objective:** Sit with Nell. (Tap the bench.)

**Nell:** Twenty years Bram's had the inn. Did you know I was the first one through the door?

**Nell:** He came down the Shardlands road in November with his coat off. He had you wrapped in it.

**Nell:** Paid for the inn in gold, there and then. Nobody in Ashford had ever seen gold.

**Nell:** The sky had been white all that night, out over the Shards. Like a lamp somebody'd left on.

**The player's choice:** He says he found me on the road. / He's never told me.

**Nell (on the road):** Does he. _(She watches the dancers for a while.)_ Well. It's a long road.

**Nell (never told):** No. He wouldn't. Some men carry a thing so long they forget it's heavy.

**Nell:** Stay near him tonight. I don't know why I'm saying it. Old women get notions.

**Nell (afterwards):** Go on with you. Tam's saving me a dance, and I've to think of a reason to refuse him.

### P1 · Tilly and the Lantern Moth

_Tilly, seven, runs up holding a card in both hands._

**Tilly:** It's a Lantern Moth. My dad found it in the barley. It's mine.

**Tilly:** It won't wake up for me. Corin says only Attuned can wake them up. He says you're Attuned.

**The player's choice:** Let me try. / It'll wake for you one day.

**Objective (let me try):** Wake the Lantern Moth. (Tap the card.)

_A moth made of lamplight unfolds out of the card. It does not go to Tilly. It circles you, twice, and settles on the back of your hand, and only goes back into the card when you hand it back to her._

**Tilly (let me try):** ...It likes you better.

**Tilly (let me try):** That's all right. Corin says it's not a person. It's a Spark.

**Tilly (one day):** Corin says I'm not.

**Narration (one day):** The card in her hands brightens as you lean in. Not a flicker. Steady, the way a candle burns when every door is shut.

**Tilly (one day):** Look. It does that when you're near.

**Tilly (afterwards):** It's still glowing. It never glows this long.

### P1 · Ned the fiddler and Hob

**Ned:** This reel's as old as the Age, they say. Somebody stood on this green and played it the last time the sky changed hands.

**Ned:** I do wonder how his night went.

**Hob:** Barley's in. Roof's patched. If the Age wants to end, it can end tonight.

**Hob:** Cabbages came up early this year. Ploughed three Sparks up with them. Can't think why.

### P1 · Corin, Bram's deck and the Lantern Duel

_Corin holds court at the edge of the green, two worn card boxes under his arm and a ring of younger kids around him. His mam watches from the bakery door. As he spots you, Bram gets up off his stool by the Lantern with a worn card box in his hand, walks over, and stops a few steps off, listening._

**Corin (when you come near):** There they are. Ashford's other Attuned. Come to lose in front of everyone?

**Corin:** Half the green's waiting. I told the other half to hurry up.

**The player's choice:** Let's duel. / What's that letter?

**Corin (what's that letter):** This? _(He glances at the bakery door and lowers his voice.)_ Ironvault's taking two Attuned from every village it lends to. Notaries.

**Corin (what's that letter):** They write the contracts, and they duel anyone who won't honour one. Room, board and twenty crowns a month. I start in spring.

**Corin (what's that letter):** Mam doesn't know.

**The player's choice:** You'd work for Ironvault? / Good for you.

**Corin (work for Ironvault):** Somebody's going to. Might as well be someone from here. _(beat)_ Don't look at me like that. You'd go too, if Bram ever let you out of his sight.

**Corin (good for you):** Yeah? _(beat)_ Yeah. Thanks.

**Corin:** I put your name down as well. They want two. Don't tell Bram.

**Corin:** Anyway. Lantern Duel. Winner lights the Great Lantern at midnight. Loser carries the drum home.

**Corin:** Only you've no deck, have you. Every Spark in Ashford sits up when you walk past, and Bram's never once let you hold one.

**Corin:** So I win by default. Again.

_Bram steps in beside you, the card box in his hand._

**Bram:** Nobody wins anything by default while I'm stood here.

**Bram:** I've kept these from you since you were small. That was for my sake, not yours. Tonight seems a fair night to stop.

**The player's choice:** Why now? / Mine? To keep?

**Bram (why now):** Because tomorrow's a new Age, and I'd rather you met it with something in your hands.

**Bram (to keep):** To keep. Twelve Sparks out of the old box. I picked every one.

_He holds the box out and you take it. The twelve cards fan out across the screen: YOUR FIRST DECK, Bram's Spark deck._

**Bram:** Anyone can burn a Sigil's magic. A Rite, cast once and gone, answers Tam as quick as it answers you.

**Bram:** Calling a Totem up out of a card, so it stands and fights for you: that takes the Attuned. In Ashford, that's you, Corin, and an old innkeeper who should know better.

**Corin:** You? Since when?

**Bram:** Since before you were born, lad.

**Bram:** Want me calling your turns from the stool, Sprout? No shame in it.

**The player's choice:** Talk me through it. / I've watched enough duels.

**Bram (talk me through it):** Right. Listen for me.

**Bram (watched enough):** Then I'll keep quiet. Mostly.

**Corin (if you talk to him again with the deck in hand):** Deck in hand and everything. Come on, before you think better of it.

**Corin:** Half of Ashford's watching. And don't go easy on me. I'd know.

**Bram:** One Totem each. And nobody sets fire to a stall this year.

**Corin:** That was one stall.

**DUEL · The Lantern Duel** · Corin · Ashford Sparks. _Duel rule: Showboat. Corin's Cinderpup always attacks the moment it can, whatever's in its way. If Bram is coaching, the duel is dealt in order and every step is gated; if not, the cards are tuned so Corin never reaches your Life. Either way it can't be lost._

**Corin (his first Fate roll):** Six. Give me a six.

**Corin (your hit lands on his weakness):** Water. Course you'd bring water.

**Corin (his Cinderpup is knocked out):** Cinderpup! Get up!

**Corin (when you win):** Fine. Fine! You were better. Tonight.

_He looks at the crowd, then at you, and the grin comes back a beat late._

**Corin:** Go on, then. You light it.

**Corin (if you'd said you'd watched enough duels):** Again? Bram's not watching this one. Loser carries the drum.

**The player's choice (if he asks):** Go on, then. / Lantern first.

_(Go on, then.) An optional duel, the same decks, nothing locked and nothing tuned: this one can be lost, and the loser carries the drum home at midnight. Whoever wins, the story goes on the same way._

**Corin (if he wins the rematch):** Evens! _(He hands you the drum, grinning.)_ Mind, it's heavy.

**Corin (if you win the rematch):** Two. _(He picks up the drum himself.)_ All right. Two.

_Tilly squeezes through the crowd at a run, her Lantern Moth glowing in her cupped hands. She stops in front of you and holds it out. As she says "You keep it", the light passes from her hands into yours, and the card shows on screen: NEW SIGIL, Tilly's Lantern Moth._

**Tilly (if you woke her moth):** For you. It was always trying to get to you anyway. You keep it.

**Tilly (if you didn't):** For luck. It glows when you're near. You keep it.

**Bram:** In your pack with it. It'll keep.

### P1 · Bram during the Lantern Duel (the tutorial)

_Only if you asked Bram to coach. An explanation stays up until you tap; an instruction lights the one thing to tap while everything else is locked. Bram never says where anything is on the screen; the light shows it._

**Bram (before the first turn):** Right. Those cards are Sigils. The gods' magic is always leaking into the world, a drop at a time, and where it lands it sets hard. Like frost on a window.

**Bram:** Yours are Sparks, the smallest kind. That's the gods on a quiet day. When they fight, it doesn't drip. It pours.

**Bram:** There's more to duelling than one night's worth. Tonight, just the basics. The rest comes as the cards do.

**Bram (Corin's Life lights up):** Your Totems do the fighting. Bring his Life down to nothing and you've won.

**Bram (the die lights up; both dice roll big in the middle of the Circle, and you win the roll):** Roll for who goes first. Highest starts.

**Bram (your first turn; your hand lights up):** That's your hand. Five to start, and one more every turn after this.

**Bram (Brookfin lights up):** Brookfin's your Totem: a Spark of river water. Pick it up.

**Bram (the card panel lights up):** That's the card up close: what it costs, what it is, what its moves do. You can look at any card like that, his or yours.

**Bram (the slot lights up):** Now call it into the Circle.

**Bram (your Essence lights up):** That cost one Essence. You get another every turn, and what you don't spend is gone, so spend it.

**Bram:** Nobody attacks on the first turn, and a Totem that's just been called needs a moment to find its feet. That's you done.

**Bram (End Turn lights up):** End your turn.

**Bram (Corin calls his pup):** There's his Cinderpup. Fire. Remember it.

**Bram (your second turn):** You drew one. Every turn, like I said.

**Bram (Brookfin lights up):** Brookfin can fight now.

**Bram (Splash lights up):** Splash costs nothing. Undertow costs one Essence, hits harder and mends Brookfin a little. Splash first.

**Bram (the Cinderpup lights up):** At his Cinderpup.

**Bram (the hit lands on its weakness):** See that? Water puts fire out. Hit a Totem where it's weak and it hurts a good deal more. The sign on every card says what it fears.

**Bram (Bulwark lights up):** You've still two Essence. Not every card's a Totem. That Bulwark's a Rite: magic you cast once, then it's gone.

**Bram:** It throws a wall of stone round one of your Totems. The next hit it takes, the stone takes first, up to fifty of it.

**Bram (the Cinderpup lights up):** He'll go straight for Brookfin on his turn. He always does. So the wall goes up now.

**Bram (Bulwark lights up):** Bulwark.

**Bram (Brookfin lights up):** Round Brookfin.

**Bram (End Turn lights up):** That'll do. End it.

**Bram (Corin rolls the die):** That's Fate. Some moves roll the die: high, and they hit hard; low, nothing. His deck's full of them.

**Bram (the Bulwark takes the hit):** And the stone took the lot.

**Bram (your third turn):** His pup's hurting. Undertow will finish it.

**Bram:** Brookfin. / Undertow. / His Cinderpup.

**Bram (the Cinderpup is knocked out):** Out it goes, back into its card. Nobody bleeds inside a Circle. That's the law.

**Bram (Corin's Life lights up):** Nothing stands across from Brookfin now. Next turn it goes straight at Corin.

**Bram (End Turn lights up):** End your turn.

**Bram (Corin's Rite gets through):** He's throwing Rites now. Brookfin's tougher than it looks.

**Bram (your fourth turn):** Now. Brookfin. / Undertow. / Straight at him. Nothing's in the way.

### Bram, the first time each thing appears (any duel he's at: Dask's road duel, the practice round)

The Lantern Duel teaches only what's on the table that night. Everything else Bram explains once, the first time it happens, in the same gated way, whoever is across the Circle (Dask, the practice round, Hale, Wren). Every line he gives is kept on the pause menu's How to Duel page, in order, with the full element chart.

**Bram (the first Fate roll, if he didn't coach the Lantern Duel):** That's a Fate roll. High, and it hits hard; low, nothing. The die doesn't care who you are.

**Bram (the first condition or boon on either side; the game fills in the Totem, the condition, what it does and how long):** His Cinderpup's Burned now. It takes 20 at its owner's Dawn. Two turns, then it's gone.

**Bram (the other side sets its first Ward):** A Ward. Face down, so you can't read it. It springs by itself when the right thing happens: a Totem called, a Rite cast, a hit coming in. Mind what you give it.

**Bram (you set your first Ward):** Yours is down. It'll spring on its own when its moment comes. Set them early, while they're cheap.

**Bram (the first Ward springs):** There it goes. It's spent now. Next time, think what it's waiting for, and don't give it that.

**Bram (the first hit on a weakness, if he didn't coach the Lantern Duel):** See that? Every element hurts something and fears something. Twelve of them. You'll learn the rest the way I did: by getting hit.

**Bram (the other side's first Guardian):** That's a Guardian. Everything goes through it first. Break it, or go nowhere.

**Bram (the first Divine Gift, either side):** A Gift. A god's favour, bought with kneeling. Nobody gets many.

**Bram (the first card taken from your hand):** She's had a card off you. Don't sit on what you can play now.

**Bram (the first time you could Ascend; the game fills in the cards):** That Blazehound is an Ascension: Cinderpup, grown up. Play it on your Cinderpup and it steps into the same slot, bigger, with everything it had. It costs Essence, so think about when.

**Bram (the first time a Summon is in your hand; the game fills in the card and its cost):** That Pyraxis is a Summon. Not a Totem: a piece of something far bigger. It costs 7 Essence, does one great thing, and goes back where it came from. Save it for when it counts.

**Bram (the first time the other side calls a Summon):** That was a Summon. One great blow, then it's gone, and it cost a fortune in Essence. Nobody can do that often.

_When Bram is across the Circle (the practice round), these lines speak of his own cards: "That's my Ward, face down…", "My Cinderpup's Burned now…", "That was my Summon…"._
